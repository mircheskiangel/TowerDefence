# Tower Defense 2D

A minimal two-level 2D tower defense game built in Unity for an "AI in Video Games" course.
Enemies walk from a spawn cell to a goal cell along the shortest path found by **A\***, and they
re-plan their route every time the player builds a tower. Towers pick their targets with
**switchable targeting strategies** (First, Closest, Strongest, Weakest).
The board is drawn in an **isometric view** by default; press **V** to switch to the flat top-down view
(the game plays exactly the same in both).

## How to build and run

1. Open the project folder in Unity Hub with Unity **6000.5.2f1**.
2. In the menu bar choose **Tower Defense > Build Level**.
3. Press **Play** (the scene `Assets/Scenes/TowerDefense.unity` is opened by the builder).

The game opens on the level select screen, in the isometric view. **Tower Defense > Build Level
(Top-Down Start)** builds the same game but starts in the top-down view (only the start view differs;
V switches in both). **Tower Defense > Unlock All Levels** and **Tower Defense > Reset Progress** set
or clear the saved unlock.

### Why the level is generated by a script

The whole level is created by an editor script, [`Assets/Editor/LevelBuilder.cs`](Assets/Editor/LevelBuilder.cs).
It generates the placeholder sprites (PNG files filled with white pixels and tinted at runtime), the
unlit sprite material, the data assets (enemies, tower, waves), all prefabs, the scene hierarchy,
the UI canvas and every reference between components.

Scenes and prefabs are **never edited by hand**. This keeps the project reproducible and avoids
merge conflicts in Unity's scene files: running **Build Level** again creates no duplicates; it
rebuilds the scene from scratch and overwrites the generated assets in place (their GUIDs are kept,
so references stay valid). All balance numbers are set in `LevelBuilder.cs`.

## Game rules

The numbers below are Level 1; [Levels](#levels) describes Level 2.

- **Grid:** 16 × 10 cells, 1 world unit per cell. The **spawn** cell is on the left edge (blue, cell (0,5)),
  the **goal** cell on the right edge (red, cell (15,4)).
- **Gold:** the player starts with **100 gold**. A tower costs **25 gold**. Killing an enemy gives gold
  (Grunt 4, Tank 10).
- **Lives:** the player starts with **10 lives**. Every enemy that reaches the goal costs **1 life**
  and gives no gold.
- **Tower placement:** a tower can be placed on any free cell except the spawn and goal cells, if the
  player has enough gold. Towers **block** their cell, so enemies must walk around them.
- **Path-blocked rule:** a placement that would leave **no path** from the spawn to the goal, or that
  would trap an enemy that is already walking, is **rejected**. No gold is spent and the message
  **"Path blocked!"** appears for 1.5 seconds. Placing a tower on the cell where an enemy is standing
  is allowed; that enemy simply walks out of it.
- **Waves:** 5 waves. Before each wave there is a countdown (10 s before the first, 8 s between waves).
  A wave is over when all of its enemies have spawned **and** none is left alive.
- **Win / lose:** clearing wave 5 wins ("YOU WIN!" with the lives left). Reaching 0 lives loses
  ("GAME OVER" with the wave reached). On either result the game freezes and an end panel with a
  Restart button appears.

| Enemy | Health | Speed (cells/s) | Gold | Look |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grunt | 76 | 2.3 | 4 | small yellow circle |
| Tank | 400 | 1.1 | 10 | large purple circle |

| Basic Tower | Cost | Range | Damage | Fire rate | Projectile speed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | 25 | 2.5 cells | 10 | 1.5 shots/s | 8 cells/s |

| Wave | Enemies |
|---|---|
| 1 | 5 Grunts, one every 1.5 s |
| 2 | 8 Grunts, one every 1.2 s |
| 3 | 6 Grunts every 1.2 s, then (after 2 s) 2 Tanks every 3 s |
| 4 | 10 Grunts every 1.0 s, then (after 2 s) 4 Tanks every 2.5 s |

Visual feedback only (no effect on gameplay): enemies flash white when hit, a small health bar
appears above an enemy after its first hit (green → yellow → red), and a tower briefly grows by 10%
when it fires.

| Walls | none | 12 cells: x=4 y=3..6; x=8 y=4..7; x=11 y=1..4 |
| Gold, lives | 100, 10 | 200, 10 |
| Waves | 5 (table above) | 3 (table below) |

| Level 2 wave | Enemies |
|---|---|
| 1 | 10 Grunts every 1.0 s, then (after 2 s) 2 Tanks every 3 s |
| 2 | 14 Grunts every 0.9 s, then (after 2 s) 4 Tanks every 2.5 s, health × 1.3 |
| 3 | 20 Grunts every 0.7 s, then (after 2 s) 7 **elite Tanks** every 2 s, health × 2.0 |

**Permanent walls.** `GridManager` keeps a permanent flag per cell. A permanent cell is blocked for
A\*, cannot be built on, sold or replaced, and `SetBlocked`, `SetBlockedSilently` and the placement
simulation with its restore step never touch it. The dirt road never draws on one. Clicking a wall shows
"Cannot build on a wall", clicking a tower shows "A tower is already here".

**Wall art.** In isometric a wall is a low boulder of 2-3 stones (`Wall_0..2`, chosen per cell from its
coordinates, with `WallShadow`): about 44 px wide (70% of a tile) and 15-17 px high, darker than the
decorative rocks and with a lighter top edge, depth sorted like a tower. It is lower than every unit, so
nothing can be completely hidden behind one and no transparency fade is needed. In top-down a wall is a
stone-coloured rounded square of 70% of a cell.

**Elite Tank.** A wave group has a `healthMultiplier` (default 1.0, so Level 1 is unchanged) and an `elite`
flag, which changes only the look: the elite ogre is drawn 1.2 times larger in darker purple with
gold-trimmed plates (`TankEliteWalk`, `TankEliteCharacterShadow`), with a wider health bar. In Balls
style it is a larger ball with a golden rim (`TankElite`, `TankEliteShadow`). Its strength comes only
from the multiplier; base stats and gold stay those of the Tank.

**Unlock.** Level 2 is locked until Level 1 has been won: losing never unlocks anything. The unlock is
stored in PlayerPrefs (the game keeps working for the session if that fails). A locked level shows a lock
icon and "Win Level 1 to unlock", and its button cannot be clicked. The win panel on Level 1 offers **Next
level**, the one on Level 2 **Menu**; **Restart** reloads the same level. BalanceBot plays any level and
never changes the progress.

**A\* overlay (O).** Shows the closed set of the last spawn-to-goal search as translucent floor cells in
both views, with the expanded count and path length in the message panel. It reads what `PathDebugView`'s
search left behind and never runs A\* itself; while it is off it does nothing at all.

- `GameManager.StateChanged` → `GameOverPanel` shows the end screen.
- `TargetingSettings.ModeChanged` → every `Tower` re-scans at once, `TargetingHUD` redraws.

## Isometric view

The view is a pure **presentation layer**. All gameplay code (A\*, movement, ranges, targeting,
projectiles, waves) works in **logical grid coordinates** (cx, cy), where integer values are cell
centers and all distances are in grid units. Only the conversion to the screen changes, so both views
play identically (the balance bots give the same results in both, see below).

`GridManager` owns an `IGridProjection` (`TopDownProjection` or `IsometricProjection`) and every
script converts through it (`GridToWorld` / `WorldToGrid` / `SortKey`). The isometric projection
uses 2:1 diamond tiles, 1 world unit wide and 0.5 high:

```
world.x = (cx - cy) * 0.5        cx = world.x + 2 * world.y
world.y = (cx + cy) * 0.25       cy = 2 * world.y - world.x      (exact inverse, used for mouse picking)
```

**Draw order.** In isometric, things further down the screen are nearer the viewer and must be drawn
on top. Each projection defines a depth key:

```
SortKey = -(cx + cy)        larger = nearer the camera = drawn in front
```

`DepthSorter` turns it into an explicit `sortingOrder` for everything that stands on the board
(towers, enemies, projectiles, decoration, portal and gate):

```
sortingOrder = 100 + (depthIndex * 20 + tieSlot) * 4 + part
depthIndex   = round(SortKey * 10) + 265         (cx + cy from -2.5 to 26.5: board + ring)
tieSlot      = floor(cx + 1.5)                   (one slot per cell column, cx -1.5 .. 18.5)
part: shadow 0, base / body 1-2, health bar frame 2, bar fill 3
z            = -cx * 0.001 + cy * 0.00001        (final tie-break, isometric only)
```

- `depthIndex` gives depth steps of 0.1 cell over the whole island, so nothing is ever clamped
  (orders 100..23379).
- `tieSlot` breaks ties between objects at the **same depth** (same cx + cy, side by side on the
  screen): the one in the higher cell column is drawn in front.
- Two objects in the same depth step **and** the same column get the same order; for them the tiny
  `z` offset decides (Unity sorts equal orders by distance to the camera): larger cx in front, then
  smaller cy. So no pair can ever swap from frame to frame. In top-down z stays 0.
- `part` keeps an object's own pieces in order (shadow under body, health bar over body).
- All orders lie above the floor, road, range ellipse and highlights (orders -2..20). The two
  overlays, a dim copy of the path line and the hover outline, use order 32700, above every unit.

Explicit orders were chosen over Unity's custom transparency sort axis because that axis is a global
renderer setting (it would also change the top-down view) and it sorts each sprite by its own pivot,
which would put a health bar behind its own enemy. In top-down every renderer keeps its original
type-based order.

Isometric-only visuals (all generated in code, like everything else, see the next section): the
grass island with a decorative ring, cliffs and a dirt road, pixel-art towers and enemies, the range
**ellipse**, projectiles flying at a small height, a thin hover outline and the metal-framed HUD.

## Skins and pixel art

All isometric art is **generated in code** by editor scripts; there are no imported images, so the
project needs no external art licences. `Tower Defense > Build Level` writes the PNG files and fills
the skin:

- `Assets/Editor/PixelArt.cs`: the toolbox. Seeded hash and value noise (the same hash formula as the
  reference script), palettes, ramp shading, polygon / ellipse / sphere drawing on a pixel canvas.
  `SaveSprite` writes `Assets/Art/Generated/<name>.png` and imports it as a pixel-art sprite: Point
  filter, no compression, no mipmaps, **64 pixels per unit** (a 64 x 32 px diamond = the 1 x 0.5 unit
  tile), pivot at the center for tiles and at the feet for standing objects, optional 9-slice border.
- `Assets/Editor/IsoArt.cs`: the drawings, ported from `Reference/skins_preview_reference.py`: grass
  (16 tiles whose noise is periodic over 4 x 4 cells, so tiles join seamlessly) plus 8 tuft/flower
  overlays picked per cell with a non-periodic hash, dirt road tiles, cliffs, trees, rocks, bushes,
  portal (with a separate pulsing glow), gate, backdrop, tower, Grunt and Tank balls with contact
  shadows, projectile, health bar frame, HUD panel, button and icons.
- **Fixed seeds** everywhere (no `UnityEngine.Random`), so a rebuild writes byte-identical PNGs.
- `Assets/Art/DefaultSkin.asset` is a `SkinSet` (`Assets/Scripts/Skins/`): named slots for grass,
  grass details, path, cliffs, portal, portal glow, gate, backdrop, decoration (trees, rocks,
  bushes), tower body / base / shadow, Grunt, Tank and their shadows, the character slots `GruntWalk`,
  `TankWalk` (2 walk frames each), `GruntCharacterShadow`, `TankCharacterShadow`, projectile, health
  bar frame, placement puff, HUD parts, and the level slots `Wall`, `WallShadow`, `TankEliteWalk`,
  `TankEliteCharacterShadow`, `TankElite`, `TankEliteShadow`, `IconLock`. `SkinLoader` fills the slots from the PNGs by file name.
- **Every slot is optional.** An empty slot falls back to the plain M7 look for that part, and the
  top-down view never uses skins.

**Replacing generated art with hand-drawn art:** draw a PNG with the same size and pivot (for example
`Assets/Art/Generated/TowerBody.png`, 64 x 52 px, feet at pixel (32, 44)), and either assign it to
the slot in `DefaultSkin.asset` or remove the `IsoArt` call for that sprite so the builder does not
overwrite it. Keep 64 pixels per unit so it matches the rest of the art.

**Decorative ring.** One cell of scenery around the 16 x 10 board (darker grass, trees, rocks and
bushes placed with a fixed seed, never on the cells next to the portal and the gate, only low decor
on the two front edges). Ring cells are **not part of the grid**: not clickable, not walkable, not
blocked, invisible to A\* and to tower placement.

**Dynamic road.** `RoadView` draws dirt tiles on the cells of the current spawn -> goal path (the same
`PathDebugView.CurrentPath` the debug line shows) plus the ring cells in front of the portal and the
gate. It redraws only when the path changes (after `GridChanged`) or the view is switched; it never
runs A\* itself.

**HUD (isometric).** `IsoHud` replaces the text labels with metal-framed panels: gold and lives with
icons, `WAVE 1 / 5`, the targeting selector with arrow buttons, a tower card (icon and price) and a
bottom message panel ("Path blocked!", "Cannot build on a wall", the wave countdown). Top-down keeps
the original labels.

**Enemy characters (isometric).** The Grunt is a goblin (yellow skin, brown tunic, pointed ears,
dagger) and the Tank an ogre (purple skin, steel shoulder plates, belt, tusks, round shield), ported
from `Reference/enemy_skins_reference.py` into `IsoArt` and saved as `GoblinWalk_0/1`, `OgreWalk_0/1`,
`GoblinShadow` and `OgreShadow`. All frames face right, with the pivot at the feet.

- **Scale:** drawn at the native 64 pixels per unit, so they share the pixel grid of the rest of the
  art: about 26 x 25 px for the goblin and 37 x 36 px for the ogre, against 21 and 33 px for the balls.
- **Skin style:** `EnemySkinSettings` holds the style (`Characters` by default, or `Balls`); the
  **K** key switches it. The balls keep their own slots. Top-down always draws the plain circles.
- **Walk animation:** `EnemyWalkAnimator` changes only the visual child. Frames follow the distance
  walked, so the rate scales with speed and a standing enemy stops animating: one frame lasts 0.18 s
  (Grunt) or 0.30 s (Tank) at normal speed. The body bob is part of the frames; balls keep `EnemyBob`.
- **Facing:** the sprite is mirrored when the projected movement goes left on screen, which in
  isometric means both +cy and -cx. A move with no horizontal part keeps the current facing.
- **Gameplay:** radius, speed, health and gold are unchanged, and the root transform never animates.

Small visual-only effects (isometric): hit flash (light red tint on the character or ball), a short
fade when an enemy dies (the copy keeps the current frame and flip), a slow pulse of the portal glow,
a dust puff when a tower is placed, and a slight bob of the walking balls.

The `Reference/` (art reference) and `Screenshots/` folders are git-ignored.

## AI techniques used

### a) A\* pathfinding

*File: [`AStarPathfinder.cs`](Assets/Scripts/Pathfinding/AStarPathfinder.cs), [`MinHeap.cs`](Assets/Scripts/Pathfinding/MinHeap.cs)*

- **The search space** is the 16 × 10 cell grid. From each cell an enemy can move in **4 directions**
  (up, right, down, left); every step costs 1. Blocked cells (towers) are not walkable.
- **Scoring:** each cell gets a score **F = G + H**. **G** is the number of steps from the start so far,
  **H** is the estimated number of steps still needed. A\* always expands the open cell with the
  lowest F next.
- **Heuristic: Manhattan distance**, H = |dx| + |dy|. On an empty 4-direction grid this is exactly
  the number of steps needed, and obstacles can only make the real path longer. So the heuristic
  **never overestimates** (it is *admissible*), which guarantees that A\* returns a **shortest**
  path, while still pulling the search straight towards the goal.
- **Open set as a binary heap:** the cells waiting to be expanded are kept in a min-heap, a tree
  stored in a flat list (children of item *i* are at *2i+1* and *2i+2*). Adding a cell "bubbles it up",
  taking the best cell "sinks" the last item down from the root. Both take **O(log n)** instead of
  scanning the whole list.
- **Tie-break on H:** when two cells have the same F, the one with the smaller H (closer to the goal)
  wins. This expands fewer cells and gives straighter paths. On the empty grid A\* expands exactly
  the 17 cells of the path and nothing else.
- **Lazy deletion:** when a cheaper way to reach a cell is found, the cell is simply pushed again
  with the better score. Older, worse copies are skipped when they come out of the heap because the
  cell is already "closed". This avoids a costly "decrease key" operation.
- **Complexity:** with V cells and E neighbour links, the search is **O(E log V)** in the worst case.
  Here V = 160 and E ≤ 4V, so one search is very cheap.
- **Result:** a list of cells **from the start to the goal, both included**, or **null** if the goal
  cannot be reached. The path is rebuilt by following "came from" links backwards from the goal.

### b) Re-pathing

*File: [`Enemy.cs`](Assets/Scripts/Enemies/Enemy.cs)*

- Every enemy subscribes to `GridManager.GridChanged`. Whenever a tower is placed, the event fires
  once and **every walking enemy re-runs A\* from the cell it currently occupies** to the goal.
- An enemy is usually between two cell centers. If it is already on its way towards the next cell of
  the new path, it keeps walking forward; otherwise it first walks back to the center of its own cell
  (at most half a cell). It never teleports.
- **The start cell is always treated as walkable**, even if it is blocked: A\* never checks the start
  cell, only its neighbours. So an enemy standing on a cell where a tower has just been built simply
  walks out of it.
- If there is no path at all, the enemy stops at its cell center and waits; the next `GridChanged`
  makes it try again. (With the path-blocked rule below this cannot normally happen.)

### c) Path-blocked rejection

*File: [`TowerPlacer.cs`](Assets/Scripts/Towers/TowerPlacer.cs)*

Before a tower is really built, the placement is **simulated**:

1. The cell is marked as blocked with `GridManager.SetBlockedSilently`, which does **not** raise `GridChanged`.
2. A\* is run from the **spawn** to the goal, and from the **current cell of every walking enemy** to the goal
   (using the same "start cell is always walkable" rule as re-pathing). Enemies that were already
   waiting without a route before the click are ignored.
3. The cell is restored to its old value in a `finally` block, so the grid always ends up exactly as before.
4. If any of these searches finds no path, the placement is rejected: no gold is spent, no tower is
   created, and "Path blocked!" is shown.

**Why `GridChanged` must not fire during the simulation:** that event means "the grid really changed".
If it fired, every enemy would re-path for a tower that does not exist, and a second time when the
cell is restored; enemies could be pushed onto useless detours and the debug path would flicker. The
silent setter keeps the experiment invisible to the rest of the game.

The same simulation colours the hover highlight red over cells that would block the path. It only
runs when the hovered cell changes (or the grid really changes), not every frame; a click always runs
the full check again.

### d) Target selection (strategy pattern)

*Files: [`Towers/Targeting/`](Assets/Scripts/Towers/Targeting), [`Tower.cs`](Assets/Scripts/Towers/Tower.cs)*

- `ITargetSelector` is the strategy interface: "given the enemies in range, which one do I attack?".
  Each targeting mode is its own small class, so the tower just calls the current selector and never
  needs a switch over modes; adding a mode means adding one class.
- The four selectors share one base class that gives every enemy a **score**, keeps the highest one,
  and breaks ties by the **smaller distance to the tower** (so the result is always deterministic):

  | Mode | Score (higher = better target) |
  |---|---|
  | First | `PathProgress` (closest to the goal) |
  | Closest | minus the distance to the tower |
  | Strongest | current health |
  | Weakest | minus the current health |

- **`PathProgress` is defined as minus the remaining path length** (0 at the goal). It is *not* the
  distance walked: after re-pathing, an enemy that took a long detour may have walked more but still
  be further from the goal. The remaining length on its current A\* path is the honest measure of
  "furthest along".
- `TargetSelectors.Get(mode)` returns **one shared instance per mode**. Selectors hold no state, so
  they can be reused by every tower and nothing is allocated while the game runs.
- **The scan runs on a 0.1 s timer**, not every frame: finding enemies in range compares every tower
  with every enemy, and doing it ten times per second instead of every frame saves most of that
  work without a visible delay. Each tower starts with a **random first delay** so that many towers do
  not all scan in the same frame. The in-range list is reused, so scanning creates no garbage.
- The mode is **global** (`TargetingSettings`); pressing T changes it for all towers, including towers
  placed later, and they re-scan immediately.

### e) Safe handling of moving targets

*Files: [`Projectile.cs`](Assets/Scripts/Towers/Projectile.cs), [`Enemy.cs`](Assets/Scripts/Enemies/Enemy.cs)*

- **Projectiles home in** on the target's current position and remember it every frame as the
  *last known position*. If the target dies or is destroyed mid-flight, the projectile drops the
  reference and keeps flying to the last known position, then disappears **without dealing damage**.
  A 3-second lifetime removes it in any other case. Unity's overloaded `==` treats a destroyed
  object as null, so no null-reference error can happen.
- **Enemies die exactly once:** the first fatal hit sets `IsDead` *before* anything else happens
  (event, gold, `Destroy`). Any later hit, even from projectiles arriving in the same frame, is
  ignored, so the gold reward can never be paid twice. The enemy also leaves `EnemyRegistry`
  immediately, so no tower can target it on its next scan.
- Reaching the goal is not a death: it costs a life and gives no gold.

## Balance and evaluation

The level was balanced with an editor-only test tool, [`BalanceBot.cs`](Assets/Editor/BalanceBot.cs).
In Play mode it plays the level automatically at 4× speed with four simple strategies, using only
earned gold and the **same placement rules** as a human (it calls `TowerPlacer.TryPlaceTower`):

- **none:** builds nothing.
- **weak:** builds exactly 4 towers in one clump next to the path.
- **reasonable:** spends gold as soon as possible on towers in pairs on both sides of the path.
- **maze:** builds walls with alternating gaps so that A\* has to route the enemies along a longer, snake-shaped path.

(To run it: enter Play mode and call `TowerDefense.EditorTools.BalanceBot.StartBatch("none,weak,reasonable,maze", 2);`
results appear in the Console and in `Logs/BalanceBot.txt`.)

### Values changed during balancing

| Value | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Starting gold / lives | 100 / 10 | 100 / 10 (unchanged) |
| Basic Tower | 25 g, range 2.5, damage 10, 1.5 shots/s | unchanged |
| Grunt health / speed / gold | 30 / 2.0 / 10 | **76 / 2.3 / 4** |
| Tank health / speed / gold | 120 / 1.0 / 25 | **400 / 1.1 / 10** |
| Waves 1–5 | as in the table above | unchanged |
| Total gold from kills | 710 | **284** |

Before balancing every strategy except "none" won with all 10 lives (even 4 towers were enough).

### Results (final verification, 2 runs per strategy)

| Player | Result | Lives left | Wave reached | Towers | Gold spent | Kills |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | lost / lost | 0 / 0 | 2 / 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 / 0 |
| weak | lost / lost | 0 / 0 | 4 / 4 | 4 | 100 | 20 / 17 |
| reasonable | **won / won** | **7 / 6** | 5 | 13 | 325 | 50 / 49 |
| maze | **won / won** | **5 / 8** | 5 | 13 / 14 | 325 / 350 | 48 / 51 |

Over all bot runs made with the final values (balancing, visual-feedback and final verification
sessions), the maze player kept **8, 8, 6, 8, 8, 5, 8** lives (average **7.3**) and the reasonable
player **6, 5, 6, 7, 6, 6** lives (average **6.0**). Every one of these runs was a win.

### Top-down vs isometric (8 runs per strategy and view)

The projection must not change gameplay, so the same bots were run in both views:

| Player | View | Won | Avg lives left | Avg wave reached | Avg kills |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | top-down / isometric | 0 / 0 of 8 | 0 / 0 | 2 / 2 | 0 / 0 |
| weak | top-down / isometric | 0 / 0 of 8 | 0 / 0 | 4 / 4 | 19.1 / 19.2 |
| reasonable | top-down / isometric | 8 / 8 of 8 | 5.4 / 5.8 | 5 / 5 | 48.4 / 48.8 |
| maze | top-down / isometric | 8 / 8 of 8 | 7.8 / 7.9 | 5 / 5 | 50.8 / 50.9 |

The small differences are normal run-to-run variance (frame timing at 4x speed decides whether one
more enemy slips through); individual runs in both views cover the same range (5-6 and 5-9 lives).

### What the results show

Without towers, or with only a small clump of four, the player loses; a sensible line of towers wins
but loses a few lives, so the level is winnable but not trivial. The maze player wins with more lives
left on average while spending about the same gold: forcing a longer route with A\* keeps enemies
inside tower range for longer. During balancing this effect only appeared once enemies were fast
enough that **time spent in range** became the bottleneck; with slow enemies, plain pairs of towers
were just as good. Re-pathing is therefore not just a technical feature but a real player strategy.

### Level 2 (6 runs per strategy, isometric, 4x)

Only Level 2 values were tuned; Level 1 and the enemy base stats are unchanged. The bot plans for this
level are new code and were corrected along the way, so a row names the plan it used.

| Run | Gold | Wave 1 / 2 / 3 (Grunts + Tanks, multiplier) | none | weak | reasonable | maze |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starting values | 150 | 12+3 / 18+6 ×1.4 / 24+10 ×2.5 | lost W1 | lost W1 | lost W3 | lost W2 |
| Iteration 1 | 200 | 10+2 / 14+4 ×1.2 / 20+6 ×1.8 | lost W1 | lost W1-2 | won, 6.00 lives | lost W3 |
| **Iteration 2 (final)** | 200 | 10+2 / 14+4 ×1.3 / 20+7 ×2.0 | lost W1 | lost W2 | **won 6/6, 4.00 lives** | **won 6/6, 1.67 lives** (final plan) |
| Iteration 3 (not used) | 200 | 8+2 / 12+4 ×1.3 / 16+9 ×2.0 | lost W1 | lost W2 | won 5/6, 1.50 lives | lost W3 |

**The targets are only partly met.** None and weak always lose, reasonable wins with few lives, and the
third wave is clearly the hardest: reasonable reaches it with all 10 lives and loses 6 there. But
reasonable never lost with the final values, and **maze wins with fewer lives than reasonable**, not more.
On this level a maze costs more than it gains: towers that only close a gap do not shoot, a tower deals
just 15 damage per second, and a level with a Grunt stream needs fire along the whole route from wave 1.

**Honest limitations:**
- Bot results vary a little between sessions, because each tower's first scan starts after a random
  delay; in single runs the maze player sometimes keeps fewer lives than the reasonable player, even
  though it is better on average.
- The bots are simple fixed build orders, not optimal players.
- Two levels, one tower type and two enemy types.
- Movement is 4-directional only, and there are no flying enemies (everything walks on the grid).

## Possible extensions

- **A second tower type** (e.g. slow but area damage), added as another `TowerSO` asset.
- **Diagonal movement** with 8 directions, using the octile (or Euclidean) distance as heuristic,
  since Manhattan distance would overestimate and break admissibility.
- **A flow field** (one search from the goal over the whole grid) instead of one A\* per enemy, which
  scales better with many enemies.
- **Tower upgrades** (more damage, range or fire rate for gold).
- **Flying enemies** that ignore the grid and fly straight to the goal, so mazes do not work against them.
- **Per-tower targeting modes** instead of one global mode.
